# M3 retrospective

`M3` built Core and ran v0's scope end to end against simulated providers: order orchestration (`F-020`), policy and
authorisation (`F-021`), the authenticated log, checkpoints and checks (`F-022`), notices (`F-023`), the simulated
providers and the scenario harness (`F-024`), and the threat re-check and security review (`F-025`), with the `M2` rows
that waited in its waves (`F-013`, `F-014`, `F-016`). This page says what worked, what didn't, and which lessons should
change the method. Facts come from the commits on `m3` (`2d62f1b9` when this page was written), the feature notes under
`features/`, `PLAN.md`, the contracts' changelogs, the review of #143 and the reviews on #243 and #256 to #264.

## What worked

- **A fresh reviewer per row, with fix rounds until approval.** Reviews caught real holes before merge:
  - **Outsiders counted as a party to an order.** `T-020-39`'s first review (#256) found that a `holding_check` entry
    and a refused stuck decide step each made any provider a party. `T-020-40` took two fix rounds (#260): its first
    review found only results held to a party, and the fix then exempted Core's questions by shape, which an outsider
    could forge.
  - **The override permission alone erased a cited statement** (`T-021-13`, #262): it was a superset of the erase
    permission, so the arm now asks for both.
  - **A firm requote left an order stuck** (`T-020-41`, #259, round 2's blocker): the log refused the customer's
    acceptance, and nothing new was shown.
  - **A citation slipped between an override's decision and its effect** (`T-020-42`, #264): the check after the
    effect ignored every citation, where it should read those made since the decision's receipt.
  - **A provider with several grants** (`T-022-09`, #261): `all` turned into `any` left 43 tests green.
  - **The simulation found what reviews didn't.** `T-024-08`'s seed 110 moved an exchange's conversion twice, which
    became `T-013-09` (#231, merged `3d9b6a45`).
- **Merging by the approved hash, through `m3-next` before `m3` moves.** On 2026-11-13 a merge by branch name took
  `7289ab38` where `0786e3a1` was approved; the diff against the approved head caught it, and the local merge was
  redone. After that, each merge took the approved hash and went to `m3-next` first. On 2026-11-24 `4a48ac54`
  (`T-020-39` at its approved `e23090bd`) failed the documentation check there (run 45347260058), so `m3` never held
  it. It went in as `a3f74195`, with the one-line fix `7d59b425` on top of the approved head.
- **The strict evidence table as the exit.** `T-024-09`'s ratchet against `develop` and `T-024-10`'s switch, whose
  check refuses any other change to the scope file, held the exit to named runs. `REQ-CAT-5` stayed partly met until
  the owner accepted its residual, rather than being counted closed.
- **Folding contract changes into unreleased versions, with the guard run against `develop`** (`M2`'s lesson 4).
  `core-internal` 1.0.0, router-api 4.0.0, state-machine and host 3.0.0 and provider-role 3.1.0 absorbed every
  `M3` change. Each reviewer ran the guard against `develop` ("1801 changed path(s), no violations", #263), and #143's
  review found no edit to a version `develop` holds. Finding 9 is the exception: the guard checks that a version grows,
  not that the step fits the change.

## What didn't

- **The owner's form answers of 2026-11-18 sat unapplied for five days.** On 2026-11-18 the owner asked for a platform
  explainer before the lead read on in the form. It was delivered that day, and the lead then took the owner's silence
  as a standing pause. No commit landed between `e63afbeb` (2026-11-18) and `19ded70b` (2026-11-23). Meanwhile the form
  showed the old defaults as "applied", even where they contradicted the answers, and three stand-ups reported the
  pause as a blocker.
- **"For `M4`" with no `M4` row.** Rows parked items as "for `M4`" or "`F-030`'s", and `F-030` has no row for them
  (findings 3, 5 and 6). Scenarios played callers Core doesn't have (the holding check, the executed-price report,
  terms and holds, the status query), so the table counted their rows verified (finding 4). #143's review found this
  against the owner's ruling that M0 to M3 is the whole product. `M2`'s inherited items 2, 6 and 7 are still open
  (finding 7), item 7 although `M2`'s page said it needed a row.
- **Both passes of the security review missed the read-grant hole** (finding 1, high). The sweep
  `each_permission_alone_allows_only_its_own_action` leaves out read grants and ten other permissions.
- **On 2026-11-13 the owner stopped the work over visibility.** The GitHub issues were untouched since 2026-11-06 and
  `M3` had no pull request. The 82 task issues (#61 to #142) and the draft #143 were made then. No commits landed
  from 2026-11-14 to 2026-11-16.
- **The scratchpad holding the lock script, the brief and review rules and the issue tools was wiped mid-milestone.**
  They're now in `rutent-worktrees/_tools`.
- **The disk filled twice, the shared build cache was wiped, and builds paused.** On 2026-11-17 the 16 GB machine
  filled its swap (load average 160 to 200), which repeats `M2`. The owner then let the full suite move to CI.
- **CI runners backed up, with 11 runs at once.** `ci.yml` runs on `pull_request` and on pushes to `m*`, so a row's
  draft PR and its `m3-next` merge ran the same suite: on 2026-11-24, 35 PR runs of `CI` and 19 push runs. A review on
  #261 waited over 45 minutes on its `ci` run. The contract guard on every PR into `m3` is red by design (it compares
  with `m3`), so each reviewer had to read a known-red check.
- **The local checks lacked CI's documentation check** until a link from a public item's doc to a private one failed
  CI (`1121edad`, then `7d59b425`). `M2`'s lesson 2 had put that check in every brief, but the rules file didn't
  carry it.
- **Shell slips.** zsh didn't split a word list, and a command chain didn't stop on a merge conflict, so a CI run was
  cancelled.
- **Process rules broken again** (`M2`'s lesson 2): an agent used `pkill` early in the milestone, and a reviewer
  later used `pgrep` in a wait loop.
- **Notes files conflicted on almost every `F-020` merge.** 36 merges resolved conflicts in `F-020`'s notes and 19 in
  `F-024`'s, out of 63 merges naming a `T-020` row (`27b061d3` is one). The lead resolved them with a keep-both script.

## Lessons for the method

These go to the methodology store as proposals (Narthex `REQ-CHN-18`, `XC-4`). None changes rutent's plan.

1. **A detour's pause ends when the detour ends.** Say in one line that the detour is done, and resume in the same
   turn. Silence isn't a new instruction, and "waiting to be told to resume" is never a blocker. When form answers
   are read, each item's `applied` shows its true state that day ("answer taken, not applied yet").
2. **No "for a later milestone" without a row in that milestone.** A note's gap, residual or hand-off that names a
   later milestone or feature names its row, or a form item by name. Before the milestone PR is called ready, the lead
   searches the notes for "for `M<n>`" and "`F-0xx`'s" and gives each hit a row or a form item.
3. **A scenario mustn't play a caller Core lacks without marking its evidence row partly met.** Where the assembly
   makes a call no production path makes, the row is partly met, and it names the missing caller and its owner.
4. **Durable tool locations.** The lock script, the brief and review rules and the issue tools live outside any
   session's scratchpad, in one place every brief names. A lesson lands as an edit to those files, not only as prose:
   the doc check and the `pkill`/`pgrep` rule were `M2` lessons that came back.
5. **Doc checks in local checks.** The local chain runs `RUSTDOCFLAGS="-D warnings" cargo doc` as CI does (now in
   `_tools/brief-rules.md`).
6. **PR CI only where `m3-next` doesn't cover it.** A task PR runs the checks its review needs; the full suite runs
   once per merge, on `m3-next`, so the runners aren't spent twice on one tree.
7. **Per-row notes files, or append-only sections.** Each row writes its own section in its own file (or appends
   under a fixed heading), so parallel rows don't conflict in one feature's notes.

## What `M4`'s tasks should take from it

`M4` is v0 in production: deployment only, by the owner's ruling. So the first rule for writing its tasks is that no
product work rides along unless the owner has ruled it deployment by name.
